# Eviction Process Guide

## Overview
Step-by-step guide through the eviction process from notice to possession. Covers state-specific requirements, proper notice procedures, court filing, and documentation. Designed to protect your legal position while following the law precisely.

## When to Use
- Tenant not paying rent and communication has failed
- Lease violation that warrants eviction
- Holdover tenant (lease expired, won't leave)
- Need to understand the process before starting

## Inputs
- **Property address and state**
- **Tenant name(s)** on the lease
- **Reason for eviction** — non-payment, lease violation, holdover, other
- **Lease terms** — relevant clauses
- **Payment history** — amount owed, last payment date
- **Prior notices sent** — dates and types
- **Attorney** — have one? (recommended)

## Process

### Step 1: Determine Eviction Type and Notice

| Reason | Notice Type | Typical Period | Curable? |
|--------|------------|---------------|----------|
| Non-Payment of Rent | Pay or Quit | 3-14 days (varies by state) | Yes — tenant can pay to cure |
| Lease Violation | Cure or Quit | 10-30 days (varies) | Usually yes, first offense |
| Repeated Violation | Unconditional Quit | 7-30 days | No — must vacate |
| Holdover | Notice to Vacate | 30 days (typically) | No — lease has ended |
| Criminal Activity | Unconditional Quit | 3-7 days | No |

### Step 2: Serve Proper Notice
**Critical — improper service invalidates the eviction:**
- [ ] Correct notice type for the situation
- [ ] Correct notice period for the state
- [ ] Proper service method (personal, posted + mailed, certified mail)
- [ ] Notice includes: tenant name, address, reason, amount owed (if non-payment), cure deadline, consequences
- [ ] Copy retained with proof of service (date, time, method, witness)

### Step 3: Wait for Notice Period to Expire
- If tenant cures (pays/fixes violation) → eviction stopped
- If tenant does not cure → proceed to filing
- DO NOT accept partial payment during this period without legal advice (may reset the clock)

### Step 4: File Eviction with Court
- [ ] File complaint/petition with local court (Justice of the Peace, District, etc.)
- [ ] Pay filing fee ($[varies by jurisdiction])
- [ ] Attach: lease, notice served, proof of service, ledger of amounts owed
- [ ] Court sets hearing date (typically 7-21 days)

### Step 5: Court Hearing
- [ ] Appear with all documentation
- [ ] Bring: lease, all notices, payment ledger, photos, communication records
- [ ] Judge rules: possession granted or case dismissed
- [ ] If granted: Writ of Possession issued

### Step 6: Writ of Possession / Lockout
- [ ] Writ delivered to constable/sheriff
- [ ] Tenant given final notice (24-48 hours typically)
- [ ] Constable executes writ — tenant removed
- [ ] Change locks immediately
- [ ] Document property condition (photos/video)

### Step 7: Post-Eviction
- [ ] Secure property and change locks
- [ ] Handle abandoned property per state law (storage period varies)
- [ ] File for money judgment if rent owed
- [ ] Update CRM and mind/active-portfolio.md
- [ ] Begin turnover process
